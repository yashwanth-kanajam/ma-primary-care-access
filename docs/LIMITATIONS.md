# Interpretation limits

- Physician counts represent historical headcounts, not FTEs, patient slots, open panels or current availability. The selected definition excludes NPs, PAs, federal physicians, hospital residents, physicians aged 75 or older and non-primary-care specialties.
- Workforce reporting location is not a patient catchment. Cross-county flows and hospitals serving a wider area can make a resident-denominator rate misleading.
- ACS estimates span 2019-2023, including pandemic years. Pairing them with a 2023 workforce snapshot is explicit, but not exact time alignment. The 2022 sensitivity holds population fixed; it is not a trend estimate.
- County averages mask neighborhoods, towns, rural travel barriers and unequal insurance access. No individual eligibility or clinical decisions follow from this analysis.
- Island and seasonal populations require separate demand and transportation evidence. Nantucket has a low physician rate but falls outside the context-based screen; nonselection is not reassurance. Dukes can change substantially when a small number of clinicians changes.
- Poverty and age are population context, not direct measurements of unmet need. No appointments, wait times, travel times, language access or affordability measures are available in these inputs.
- A median or percentile is a relative threshold, not an accepted adequacy standard. Neither a shortlist nor scenario frequency identifies an optimal clinic site. No composite score is used.
- ACS MOEs and derived MOEs describe sampling uncertainty only. The context-bound scenarios do not vary the statewide benchmarks, capture covariance, or test county-state significance. Unchanged results in these scenarios do not eliminate uncertainty.
- Map geometry is for display: the Census cartographic boundaries generalize coastlines; polygon exteriors are plotted in longitude/latitude . Interior holes are omitted. The map is not a navigation or travel-time surface.
- The source snapshot is pinned for reproducibility. Changes to source years, definitions or checksums require a new documented analysis, not silent replacement.

Before a service-planning investment, collect current clinician FTEs, appointment waits, new-patient acceptance, insurance participation, travel/ferry constraints, subcounty indicators and seasonal population. Validate the findings with local organizations; no such stakeholder validation or implementation impact is claimed here.
